Reading a Level Like a Paper

MethodLevel design

I noticed something recently: the way I read a CHI paper and the way I take apart a level are almost the same procedure. Both are asking one question — what does the author assume the reader, or the player, knows right now?

Assumptions can be marked up

Every paragraph of a paper carries an assumption about its reader. When an author writes "as noted above," they are betting you remember a definition from three pages back. When they write "clearly," the bet is much larger.

Levels work the same way. A door that only opens if you approach it from behind is betting the player has already built the rule that doors in this world don't always open from the front. If that rule appears for the first time in this level, it isn't a puzzle. It's a trap.

My habit now is to take a sheet of paper, list every place in a level that requires the player to already know some X, and then annotate where each X was taught. More often than not, two or three of them have no source at all.

The cost of checking sets the ceiling on ambition

In a paper, a bolder claim needs stronger evidence. In a level, a bolder puzzle needs cheaper trial and error.

Those two sentences are the same sentence. Whether a player is willing to go test a hypothesis depends on what being wrong costs. When the cost is high, people don't guess — they go look it up. Just as a reviewer who meets an unsupported strong claim will not try to imagine how it might be true; they will write reject.

So if you want players to guess boldly, the move isn't to make the puzzle easier. It's to push the cost of guessing wrong down toward zero.

A half-formed conclusion

If both of those hold, then "is this level any good" might decompose into two more measurable questions:

  1. How many pieces of required prior knowledge have no source before this level?
  2. How many seconds does it cost a player to test one hypothesis?

The second is something you can read straight out of a log. I added that statistic to Playtest Logger and I'm still trying it out. The first one I can only tag by hand so far — if anyone has a better approach, I'd love to talk.
