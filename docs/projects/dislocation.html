Dislocation Communication

An emotional narrative game for a parent and a child in a single-parent family, built on the Satir family therapy model. One player describes a real conflict; the other works down through the layers of feeling underneath it. The game plays the part of a mediator that neither person has to admit they needed.

Why single-parent families

In the European Union roughly 7.8 million households with children are headed by a single parent, about 14% of households with children; in Sweden, Denmark and Estonia the share passes a quarter. In China the figure is around 39 million, close to 12% of families. The literature keeps returning to four strains: tense relations where educational values differ, communication hurdles left by an absent parent, imbalanced care that swings between neglect and indulgence, and role dislocation — a child quietly taking on emotional duties that belong to an adult.

The Satir model as a mechanic

Satir's iceberg metaphor holds that beneath surface emotions like anger or withdrawal sit unmet longings — safety, recognition, belonging. The game turns that into something you operate. One player describes an event; the system generates a layered reading of it; the other player uses an "iceberg searchlight" to work down through surface emotion, to the layer beneath, to the longing at the bottom. Making the other person's inner state into an object you have to actively examine is the whole design.

Repair as feedback

Progress is not a score. Completing a chapter's challenge restores the scene — a room that began broken is put back together. The state of the space is the feedback on the state of the relationship, which means neither player has to be told they did well; they can see it.
