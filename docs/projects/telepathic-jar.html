Telepathic Jar

Two bottles in two different places, each sensing the weather where it stands and translating it into light and mist. The point is not to report the weather. It is to give two people a quiet, ambient sense that the other is having a grey afternoon — the kind of presence a messaging app is bad at carrying.

Curating, not reporting

The system never shows a raw sensor value. Which colours the light can take, how bright it is allowed to get, and what makes the nebuliser fire are all design decisions — they map temperature, humidity, pressure, ambient light and live weather onto an atmosphere rather than a readout. The bottle form is deliberate too: the storm glass is a weather instrument with a few centuries of design heritage behind it, and this is an attempt at a modern one.

How the two jars find each other

On first boot each jar is given its own device ID and its partner's through a web configuration portal. From those it builds its MQTT topics — one to publish its readings, one to subscribe to its partner's — and connects to the broker over TLS. The first firmware simply reconnected whenever the link dropped, which failed quietly and often; the shipped version adds periodic reconnection and detailed status reporting, so a jar that has lost its partner says so instead of just going dark.

Making it

Everything was assembled and tested by hand — sensors verified one at a time against serial output before anything went into an enclosure. Low power was not the focus of this build, but the component choices leave room for it: deep sleep on the ESP32 and a battery are the obvious next step.
