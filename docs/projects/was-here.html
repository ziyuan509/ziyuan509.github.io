Was Here: From Presence to Trace

An interactive projection installation in a semi-open corridor at the centre of RMIT — a route people walk through rather than stop in. Four frames pick up the bodies passing by and translate them into four different kinds of trace: a live silhouette, a data trail, a mark on the physical environment, and finally an anonymous flow of information. The question underneath it is whether merely passing through can count as a form of presence.

Reading the site

This corridor links the main campus to the Alumni Courtyard, and it is busiest between classes. People pass through quickly; almost nobody stops. That is exactly what made it the right place to ask what a brief, forgettable presence leaves behind. Before deciding anything about the work, we mapped foot traffic, dwell time and sightlines — a projection here has to be legible at walking pace, to someone who never intended to look at it.

Four frames, four kinds of trace

The first frame captures a real-time silhouette and projects it onto the wall, where it lingers for a few seconds and fades — the body made briefly visible, with no action required. The second turns the same movement into a data trail. The later frames push further from the body: an impact registered on the physical environment, then an anonymous flow of information in which the individual is no longer legible. Read left to right, the sequence is a person being progressively abstracted by the systems that notice them.

Building it

The projector sits above a window for throw distance; the camera sits at the base of the second window so it catches people at walking height. Frames were cut from MDF for a smooth, weather-tolerant surface, then reinforced with Core Flute to stay light enough to mount in a public corridor.
