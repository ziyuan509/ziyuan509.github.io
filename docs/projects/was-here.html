Was Here: From Presence to Trace

An interactive projection installation in a corridor at RMIT that people walk through rather than stop in. Four adjacent frames read the same motion signal and represent it four different ways — a live silhouette, a data trail, a mark on the environment, and an anonymous flow. Watching them left to right is watching a person be progressively abstracted by the system that noticed them.

Holding the input constant, varying the representation

The design move is a controlled comparison. All four frames consume the same camera-derived motion signal; what changes between them is only how that signal is rendered. The first is indexical — a silhouette that maps one-to-one onto the body and fades after a few seconds. The second abstracts to trajectory: the person becomes a trail. The third records an effect on the environment that persists after the person has gone. The fourth aggregates, and the individual stops being recoverable at all.

Because the input never changes, the difference a viewer feels between frame one and frame four is attributable entirely to representation. That is the argument the piece is making: how a system chooses to depict you determines whether you remain a person in it.

Designing for walking pace

In a corridor nobody stops, so latency — not resolution — set the constraints. A silhouette is only legible as yours if it appears while you are still in front of the frame; a few hundred milliseconds late and it reads as someone else's. That pushed the projector above the window for throw distance and put the camera at the base of the second window, capturing at walking height rather than overhead, so the detection region matched the strip of floor a person actually crosses.

Passive interaction and the question of consent

Nothing here asks permission. That is deliberate and uncomfortable in the way it is meant to be: the piece reproduces the condition it is about, which is being sensed by infrastructure you did not opt into. The four frames give that condition a legible shape — you can watch yourself become a silhouette, then a trail, then a statistic, in the time it takes to walk past.
